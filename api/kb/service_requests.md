## Overview

Get It Done is the City's system for reporting non-emergency issues in public spaces -- potholes,
graffiti, streetlights that are out, illegal dumping, and dozens of other categories rolled up into
roughly 23 groups. You can submit a report through the Get It Done website, the mobile app, or by
phone, and most categories don't require you to give contact information, though leaving an email
lets you get status updates on your case. Photos aren't required for most categories, but they are
required for graffiti and illegal dumping reports so crews can assess the issue before dispatch.

Every report gets a case ID and a routing decision based on category: potholes and sidewalk issues
go to the Transportation Department's Streets Division, missed collection and bulky-item requests
go to Environmental Services, homeless encampment reports go to the Homelessness Strategies &
Solutions Department, and so on. There's no single published SLA sheet covering every category --
missed trash collection is typically handled the next business day, while a pothole or streetlight
repair can take weeks depending on crew availability and severity.

A case moves through four possible statuses: New, In Process (assigned to a department and being
worked), Referred (sent to an outside agency like Caltrans or a utility, which is typically a
terminal status with no further City updates), or Closed. A case can also be marked Duplicate and
linked to a parent case if the same issue was already reported by someone else. You can check status
anytime using your case ID at getitdone.sandiego.gov or in the app.

Get It Done is strictly for non-emergency issues. A true emergency -- a life-safety hazard, an
active crime, a fire -- should always go to 911. Non-urgent police matters (a report that doesn't
need an immediate response) go to the police non-emergency line, not Get It Done.

## How it works

1. **Submit** via web, app, or phone with the category, address, description, and a photo if
   required for that category.
2. **Classify** the free-text description into one of roughly 65 specific service types, rolled up
   into about 23 categories.
3. **Validate**: the address must geocode inside San Diego city limits and resolve to a council
   district; graffiti and illegal-dumping reports require a photo.
4. **Route** to the owning department using the category-to-department map.
5. **Dispatch and track**: the case moves to In Process, then to Closed once resolved (or Referred
   if it belongs to an outside agency).

## FAQ

### Q: How do I report a pothole?
Submit a Pothole report through Get It Done on the web or the app, including the location and a
photo if you have one. It routes to the Transportation Department's Streets Division for repair.

### Q: How do I report graffiti?
Submit a Graffiti report through Get It Done with the address and a photo -- a photo is required
for this category so crews can assess it before dispatch. Most public graffiti is targeted for
removal within about a week.

### Q: How do I report illegal dumping?
Submit an Illegal Dumping report through Get It Done with the location and a photo. Don't attempt
to move or handle anything that looks hazardous yourself -- let the crew that's dispatched deal
with it.

### Q: How do I check the status of a report?
Use your case ID at getitdone.sandiego.gov or in the mobile app. Status will show as New, In
Process, Referred, or Closed.

### Q: What does "Referred" status mean?
It means your case was sent to an outside agency -- Caltrans, a utility, or another non-integrated
department -- because it's outside the City's direct jurisdiction. Referred is typically a terminal
status, so don't expect further status updates to come back through Get It Done.

### Q: How long will my request take to resolve?
It varies a lot by category. Missed collection is usually handled the next business day, while
potholes, streetlights, and similar infrastructure issues can take weeks depending on crew
scheduling -- there isn't one published SLA that covers every category.

### Q: Can I report an issue anonymously?
Most categories don't require any contact information to submit a report. Leaving an email address
is optional, but it's the only way to receive status updates on that specific case.

### Q: Get It Done vs. 911 or the police non-emergency line -- which do I use?
Get It Done is only for non-emergency service issues in public spaces. A true emergency always
goes to 911, and a non-urgent police matter that doesn't need Get It Done's category system should
go to the police non-emergency line instead.

### Q: A tree branch fell in the road -- how do I report it?
File a Tree Maintenance report through Get It Done at
getitdone.sandiego.gov/TSWNewReport?type=Tree+Maintenance, or ask here and it can be filed for you
under the tree_trimming category. It routes to the Parks & Recreation Department, which maintains
City street trees and park trees. Give the block or the street address where the branch came down.
If the branch is blocking a traffic lane or has brought a power line down with it, that is an
emergency -- call 911 first and file the report afterwards. A tree wholly on private property is
the property owner's responsibility, not the City's.

### Q: The sidewalk in front of my house is cracked -- who is responsible for fixing it?
Under California Streets and Highways Code section 5610 the owner of the property next to the
sidewalk is generally responsible for repairing it, which is why a cracked panel in front of a
house is usually the homeowner's to fix rather than the City's. The City repairs sidewalk damage
that a City street tree or a City facility caused. Report an uplifted, broken or hazardous panel
through Get It Done under the sidewalk_repair category; it routes to the Transportation
Department's Streets Division, which inspects it and decides who owns the repair.

### Q: How do I report a homeless encampment?
File an Encampment report through Get It Done at
getitdone.sandiego.gov/TSWNewReport?type=Encampment, or ask here and it can be filed for you under
the homeless_encampment category. It routes to the Homelessness Strategies & Solutions Department,
which sends outreach before any cleanup is scheduled. Give the nearest address or the cross
streets. An encampment on a state highway, a freeway shoulder or a state-owned underpass sits on
Caltrans property rather than City property, so that one goes to Caltrans instead and comes back
through Get It Done as Referred.

### Q: My neighbour's yard is full of junk and weeds -- who do I report that to?
Property-maintenance problems on private land -- accumulated junk, overgrown weeds, an unpermitted
room addition -- go to the Development Services Department's Building & Land Use Enforcement
division, described at sandiego.gov/development-services/building-land-use-enforcement. File the
report through Get It Done under the stro_code_enforcement category, which is the code-enforcement
route, and give the address of the property rather than your own. You do not have to speak to the
owner first; an investigator inspects the property and deals with the owner directly.

### Q: There is no curb ramp on my corner -- how do I get one?
A missing curb ramp, or any other accessibility barrier in the public right of way, is an ADA
complaint rather than an ordinary sidewalk repair. File it at getitdone.sandiego.gov/ADACA, email
adacompliance@sandiego.gov, or call the ADA Compliance office at 619-236-5979; the complaint
process is described at sandiego.gov/adacompliance/complaint. Through this assistant the report is
filed under the ada_accessibility category so it routes to ADA Compliance and not to general
sidewalk repair. Give the intersection and which corner of it, so the ramp can be located.

### Q: Can I report a noise complaint through Get It Done?
No. Loud music, a party, or construction starting before the permitted hour is not a Get It Done
category. Call the Police non-emergency line at 619-531-2000 while the noise is happening, because
an officer has to hear it to act on it. Residential quiet hours run from 10 p.m. to 7 a.m. under
San Diego Municipal Code section 59.5.04, and a repeated violation can draw a $1,000
administrative citation. The City's noise complaint page is
sandiego.gov/police/community/noise-complaints.

### Q: A stray dog is loose on my street -- is that a Get It Done report?
No. A live stray, loose, injured or aggressive animal goes to the San Diego Humane Society, which
is the City's animal control partner: call 619-299-7012 extension 1, staffed 24 hours a day. The
City's animal services page is sandiego.gov/park-and-recreation/activities/animalservices. A dead
animal is the one that is a Get It Done report -- category dead_animal_removal, which routes to
Environmental Services for a public street. If an animal is actively attacking someone, call 911.

### Q: The water from my tap is brown -- is that a Get It Done report?
No. Discoloured, cloudy or bad-smelling tap water goes to Public Utilities Customer Support at
619-515-3500, not to Get It Done. Do not drink the water until the City has checked it. Customer
support information is published at sandiego.gov/public-utilities/customer-support. A burst main,
an active leak on the street side of the meter, or a sewer backup is an emergency rather than a
quality complaint, and that goes to the 24/7 Water and Sewer Emergency Line at 619-515-3525.
