## Overview

The City's Development Services Department (DSD) issues building permits for anything from a
water-heater swap to a full custom home. Most permits are submitted through the Accela Citizen
Access (ACA) portal at aca.accela.com/SANDIEGO, along with form DS-3032 and any required
supplemental forms (DS-3042 for an owner-builder, DS-6005 for a No-Plan Permit). Fees fall into a
handful of families: a flat per-trade fee for a Simple Permit (about $100-$300, estimate), a combo
fee for a No-Plan Permit (about $411.02, estimate), and a valuation-based fee for anything that
needs plan review -- roughly $4 per $100,000 of construction value for the inspection portion
(IB-101) plus a plan-check fee of about 65-85% of the permit fee on top (IB-501, estimate).

Turnaround time depends entirely on how much review a project needs. A licensed contractor can
often walk out with a Simple Permit the same day; a No-Plan Permit typically clears in about 10
business days once the historic and pre-construction checks are done. Projects that require full
plan review -- tenant improvements, additions, ADUs -- run from 4-8 weeks (commercial TI) to 3-5
months for a standard-plan ADU, and 6-12 months for a custom ADU design. Residential solar under
38.4kW AC on a single-family home or duplex is the fast lane: SolarApp+ gives 1-5 business day
instant issuance instead of the usual 10-21 day manual review.

Every permit has a status you can track in ACA: Submitted, In Review, Issues Identified (a
reviewer found something to fix), Approved/Ready to Issue, Issued, Inspections, and finally
Finaled/Closed. Records from before 2018 live in the older OpenDSD system (opendsd.sandiego.gov)
rather than ACA. Permits expire if work doesn't start within 1 year, and the permit itself is only
valid for 3 years (single-family/duplex) or 4 years (everything else) from issuance -- Form DS-117
extends that window if you need more time.

A structure 45 years or older automatically triggers a historic review discipline alongside the
usual structural/fire/electrical/mechanical/plumbing/planning review, since San Diego's Historical
Resources Board has an interest in anything that old. Owner-builders (homeowners pulling their own
permit instead of hiring a licensed contractor) must sign the DS-3032 declaration and attach a
separate DS-3042 verification form acknowledging the added liability.

## How it works

1. **Submit** the application through Accela ACA with form DS-3032 plus any supplemental forms the
   project type requires.
2. **Extract** the key fields: approval type, project address, APN, valuation, square footage, and
   contractor license number if one applies.
3. **Completeness check**: DS-3042 must be attached if owner-builder is checked, DS-6005 if the
   approval type is No-Plan, and a signature and date must be present.
4. **Route** to the disciplines the scope touches -- structural, fire, electrical, mechanical,
   plumbing, planning -- plus historic review if the structure is 45+ years old.
5. **Plan-check cycle(s)** run per discipline; an Issues Identified status sends the application
   back to In Review until every discipline signs off.
6. Once every discipline clears, the permit is **Approved**, a valuation-based fee invoice is
   generated, and payment is collected.
7. After payment the permit is **Issued**; the applicant schedules inspections (footing, framing,
   rough MEP, and a final) through the portal, and the permit reaches **Finaled** once all required
   inspections pass.

## FAQ

### Q: Do I need a permit to remodel my kitchen/bathroom?
It depends on scope. A No-Plan Permit covers a like-for-like remodel with no structural changes --
swapping fixtures, cabinets, or finishes in place. If you're moving walls, adding square footage,
or doing major plumbing or electrical rerouting, you need a full Building Permit with Plans. See
Information Bulletin IB-203 for the exact dividing line.

### Q: How do I check my permit status?
Log into Accela ACA at aca.accela.com/SANDIEGO with your permit number to see the current status
and any open corrections. Records from before 2018 aren't in ACA -- look those up in the older
OpenDSD system at opendsd.sandiego.gov instead.

### Q: What does "Issues Identified" mean?
It means a plan-review discipline -- structural, fire, electrical, or another -- found something
that needs to be corrected before the permit can move forward. All open items across every
discipline that reviewed the project must be resolved before you resubmit; see IB-610 for the
resubmittal process.

### Q: How long does a building permit take?
Simple and No-Plan permits typically clear in days, not weeks. Solar through SolarApp+ is 1-5
business days for qualifying systems. Anything needing full plan review runs longer: about 4-8
weeks for a commercial tenant improvement, and 3-5 months (or more, for custom designs) for an ADU.

### Q: What is the fee for a building permit?
For a project with plans, expect a valuation-based inspection fee of roughly $4 per $100,000 of
construction value (IB-101, estimate) plus a plan-check fee of about 65-85% of the permit fee
(IB-501, estimate). Simple and No-Plan permits use flatter fee schedules instead -- see the permit
type list for estimates by category.

### Q: Can a homeowner pull their own permit?
Yes. As an owner-builder you sign the DS-3032 owner-builder declaration and attach a separate
DS-3042 verification form acknowledging that you understand the added liability of not using a
licensed contractor. You can still hire licensed subcontractors for individual trades.

### Q: How long is a permit valid?
A permit is valid for 3 years from issuance for a single-family home or duplex, or 4 years for
everything else. Work has to start within 1 year of issuance or the permit can lapse. If you need
more time, Form DS-117 requests an extension.

### Q: Do I need a permit for solar panels?
Yes, but qualifying residential systems move fast. A system up to 38.4kW AC on a single-family
home or duplex can go through SolarApp+ for 1-5 business day instant issuance instead of the usual
manual review, which can take 10-21 days.

### Q: Simple Permit vs No-Plan Permit -- what's the difference?
A Simple Permit is a single-trade mechanical, electrical, or plumbing job with no plan review at
all, and is often issued instantly to a licensed contractor. A No-Plan Permit is a like-for-like
repair or replacement on a structure that's already finaled, and typically takes about 10 business
days for the historic and pre-construction checks.

### Q: How much does an ADU cost and how long does it take?
Total permit and development impact fees for an ADU typically run $6,000-$20,000 (estimate); units
under 750 square feet are exempt from the impact-fee portion. Standard-plan ADUs generally take
3-5 months; a fully custom design can take 6-12 months.

### Q: Do I need a permit for a business sign?
Yes, a Sign Permit is required for a new or replacement sign. You'll need a vicinity map,
dimensions, and setbacks per IB-501/IB-111; electrical or high-rise signs also require a follow-up
inspection.

### Q: What happens after my permit is Issued?
You schedule the required inspections -- footing, framing, rough mechanical/electrical/plumbing,
and a final -- through the same portal you applied in. Every required inspection has to pass,
including the final, before the permit reaches Finaled/Closed status.
