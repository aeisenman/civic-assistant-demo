## Overview

Housing questions split into a few different lanes: short-term rentals (STRO) need a City
license on top of the business paperwork covered in `business.md`; accessory dwelling units
(ADUs) need a building permit and follow their own size rules, separate from the general permits
covered in `permits.md`; landlord-tenant disputes and rent increases are mostly State law, with
the City pointing residents to legal aid rather than adjudicating disputes itself; and the San
Diego Housing Commission (SDHC) -- a separate public agency from the City -- runs rental
assistance and affordable housing programs. Homelessness outreach and shelter referral is a City
program with its own dedicated hotline. Treat every dollar figure in this file as an estimate:
STRO fees and program benefit amounts are the kind of number a real city updates without much
notice.

## How it works

1. Figure out which lane a housing question is in: a short-term rental license (STRO), an ADU
   building question, a landlord-tenant dispute, a rental-assistance question, or a
   homelessness/shelter need -- each has a different office.
2. For STRO, confirm the tier that matches how the unit will be rented (part-time vs. home-share
   vs. whole-home) before quoting a fee, since the four tiers have different costs and rules.
3. For an ADU, check the size and parking rules, then route to the standard building permit
   process (`permits.md`) -- ADUs always need a permit, there's no exemption.
4. For a tenant dispute or rent-increase question, don't give legal advice -- point to the
   referral (Legal Aid Society of San Diego) and note the general State rent-cap and notice
   rules.
5. For rental assistance or affordable housing, point to SDHC as a separate agency with its own
   application process, not something the City processes directly.
6. For homelessness outreach or a resident needing shelter now, give the SDHC HOUSING FIRST - SAN
   DIEGO hotline number directly rather than routing through a general city line.

## FAQ

### Q: What license do I need to run an Airbnb or short-term rental?
A Short-Term Residential Occupancy (STRO) license from the City Treasurer, plus a Transient
Occupancy Tax (TOT) certificate and a Rental Unit Business Tax certificate (see `business.md`).
There are four license tiers depending on how many days a year you rent and whether you live
on-site -- see the next question for the tier breakdown and current fees.

### Q: What are the STRO license tiers and how much do they cost?
Tier 1 (part-time, renting 20 days a year or less, host doesn't need to live there): $33
application + $193 license (estimate). Tier 2 (home-sharing, host lives on-site and may be away
up to 90 days/year): $33 + $284. Tier 3 (whole-home, more than 20 days/year, host not on-site,
citywide except Mission Beach, capped by a citywide 1% housing-stock limit): $41 + $1,129. Tier 4
is the same as Tier 3 but only for the Mission Beach Community Planning Area. Tier 3/4 licenses
require at least 90 days of actual rental use per year to keep the license and file quarterly
reports.

### Q: Can I put my ADU on Airbnb?
No -- under current City policy, accessory dwelling units can't be used for short-term rentals,
regardless of STRO tier. An ADU can be rented as a regular long-term rental, just not as a
short-term one.

### Q: How big can an ADU be, and do I need a permit?
An ADU can be up to 1,200 square feet (that cap doesn't apply if you're converting an existing
structure), with a 150 sq ft minimum. A Junior ADU (carved out of an existing house or attached
garage) runs 150-500 sq ft. A Building Permit is always required for either -- there's no
exemption -- and coastal properties may also need a Coastal Development Permit. Plan check for a
standard ADU typically runs 4-8 weeks (estimate); using a City pre-approved plan set can qualify
for a faster, roughly 30-day track.

### Q: Does my ADU need extra parking?
Generally no, outside the Coastal Overlay Zone -- most ADUs don't require an added parking space.
Inside coastal areas, one space may be required unless the unit is 500 sq ft or smaller or
otherwise exempt. A Junior ADU never requires parking.

### Q: My landlord is raising my rent a lot -- is that legal?
California's statewide rent cap generally limits most increases to 5% plus local inflation or
10%, whichever is lower, with 30 days' written notice for an increase under 10% and 90 days' for
10% or more (estimate on the exact current percentage -- state law and your specific tenancy
details control). This isn't something the City adjudicates; for a specific dispute, contact the
Legal Aid Society of San Diego, which offers free or low-cost tenant help.

### Q: I'm being evicted or told to sign a "cash for keys" agreement -- what do I do?
Talk to a tenant attorney before you sign anything or move out. The Legal Aid Society of San
Diego runs free tenant clinics covering eviction notices and similar situations; the City itself
doesn't represent tenants or landlords in these disputes.

### Q: How do I apply for rental assistance or a Section 8 voucher?
That's run by the San Diego Housing Commission (SDHC), a public agency separate from the City,
not something processed through City Hall. SDHC's Housing Choice Voucher program serves a large
number of low-income households citywide; check sdhc.org for current waitlist status, since some
SDHC assistance programs open and close application periods and have changed over time (for
example, a security-deposit loan program that SDHC discontinued in 2026 as a cost-saving move).

### Q: I'm about to lose my housing -- who do I call right now?
Call SDHC's HOUSING FIRST - SAN DIEGO hotline at 619-578-7768. The City also runs a Homelessness
Response Center ("the Hub") that helps with housing-application navigation, and street outreach
teams (including PATH and Bridge Shelter programs) work directly with people experiencing
homelessness -- the hotline is the fastest single number to start with.

### Q: Are there City programs to help first-time homebuyers or renters with income limits?
SDHC administers several affordable-housing and homebuyer-assistance programs for
income-qualified residents; details and current eligibility change fairly often, so treat any
specific number here as unverified and check sdhc.org's current program list rather than relying
on a fixed figure from this assistant.
