## Overview

Anyone doing business within San Diego city limits for more than 6 days a year needs a Business
Tax Certificate (BTC) from the City Treasurer -- note that this is a tax registration, not a
"license" in the professional-licensing sense. The fee is tiered by employee count: 12 or fewer
employees pay a flat $34 base plus a $4 SB-1186 accessibility fee ($38 total, estimate); 13 or more
employees pay $125 plus $5 per employee plus the $4 SB-1186 fee. Both tiers add a $1.47-per-employee
Minimum Wage Enforcement Fee, effective July 1, 2025. You can apply online at
pay.sandiego.gov/BTaxApp, by mail to PO Box 122289, or in person at Civic Center Plaza -- paying
online issues the certificate immediately, while mail applications can take up to about 2 weeks.

Certificates must be filed within 15 days of your business start date. File late and you'll owe a
late fee of the greater of $25 or 10% of the tax due, plus a 1%-per-month ongoing penalty; operating
without ever paying adds a flat surcharge of $68 (12 or fewer employees) or $250 (13 or more). The
certificate renews on a recurring annual cycle -- the City sends a courtesy reminder, but the
renewal obligation exists whether or not you get the email.

A Business Tax Certificate doesn't replace other approvals your business might need. Selling
tangible goods usually requires a free Seller's Permit from the state's CDTFA. A business name that
isn't just your own legal name plus a plain description needs a Fictitious Business Name (DBA)
filing with the County Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk (valid 5 years, requires newspaper
publication). Certain activities -- tobacco retail, for example -- carry police or fire regulatory
flags on the application that route to those departments for a compliance check before the
certificate issues.

Short-term rentals (Airbnb-style) are a separate, heavier lift: you need a Short-Term Residential
Occupancy (STRO) license from the Treasurer, an active Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) certificate, a
Rental Unit Business Tax certificate, and $1M in liability insurance. Accessory dwelling units
(ADUs) cannot be used as short-term rentals under current City policy. Free one-on-one help for new
businesses is available from the City's Economic Development Dept (619-236-6700), the Small
Business Development Center (SBDC), SCORE San Diego, and the SBA San Diego District Office.

## How it works

1. **Submit** Parts 1-5 of the Business Tax Application via the BTaxApp portal, by mail, or in
   person.
2. **Extract** the key fields: ownership type, business address, employee count, business
   activity code, and any police/fire regulatory flags.
3. **Validate**: the business address can't be a PO Box and employee count must be present; if
   Part 4 is checked the application routes to SDPD for review, and if Part 5 is checked it routes
   to Fire-Rescue.
4. **Compute the fee** from the employee-count tier (12-or-fewer vs. 13-or-more) plus the SB-1186
   and Minimum Wage Enforcement fees.
5. **Payment** clears and the BTC is issued (status Active/Issued).
6. **Annual renewal** recalculates the fee each cycle; filing more than 15 days after the business
   start date (or missing a renewal) triggers the late-fee penalty schedule.

## FAQ

### Q: Do I need a business license?
The City doesn't issue "business licenses" in the traditional sense -- it issues a Business Tax
Certificate. Anyone conducting business within San Diego for more than 6 days per year is required
to have one, regardless of whether the business is home-based, online-only, or has a storefront.

### Q: How much does a Business Tax Certificate cost?
For 12 or fewer employees it's $38 total ($34 base + $4 SB-1186 fee, estimate), plus $1.47 per
employee for the Minimum Wage Enforcement Fee. For 13 or more employees it's $125 plus $5 per
employee plus the $4 SB-1186 fee, plus the same $1.47-per-employee enforcement fee.

### Q: How do I apply?
Apply online at pay.sandiego.gov/BTaxApp, by mail to PO Box 122289, or in person at Civic Center
Plaza. The online application is generally the fastest since it issues the certificate as soon as
payment clears.

### Q: How long does it take to get my certificate?
If you apply and pay online, the certificate issues immediately. Mail applications take longer --
budget up to about 2 weeks for processing and mailing back your certificate.

### Q: What if I apply late?
You're required to file within 15 days of your business start date. Filing later triggers a late
fee equal to the greater of $25 or 10% of the tax owed, plus an additional 1% per month until it's
paid, and a flat operating-without-payment surcharge can also apply.

### Q: Do I need to renew every year?
Yes, the Business Tax Certificate is on a recurring annual cycle. The City sends a courtesy
reminder before your renewal is due, but that reminder is not a legal notice -- the obligation to
renew and pay exists independent of whether you receive it.

### Q: I work from home -- do I need a certificate or a special permit?
You still need a standard Business Tax Certificate; check the Home-Based Business box on the
application. You generally don't need an additional land-use permit as long as you meet Home
Occupation rules: no on-site employees, no client visits, and the business uses no more than 25% of
your home's floor area.

### Q: Do I need to check zoning before signing a lease?
Yes -- use DSD's ZAPP tool to confirm your intended use is allowed at that address before you sign.
The Business Tax Certificate application relies on self-certified zoning compliance; the City does
not pre-check zoning for you as part of issuing the certificate.

### Q: Do I need a fictitious business name (DBA) filing?
Only if your business name is something other than your own legal surname plus a plain description
of what you do. If you need one, file it with the County Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk (ARCC) and
publish it in a newspaper; the filing is valid for 5 years.

### Q: Do I need a seller's permit?
Yes, if you sell or lease tangible goods subject to California sales tax -- including online sales
shipped from San Diego. It's issued by the state's CDTFA, is free, and is often instant to obtain
online at cdtfa.ca.gov.

### Q: What do I need for an Airbnb or short-term rental?
You need a Short-Term Residential Occupancy (STRO) license from the City Treasurer, an active
Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) certificate, and a Rental Unit Business Tax certificate, plus $1M in
liability insurance. Accessory dwelling units (ADUs) are not eligible to be used for short-term
rentals.

### Q: Where can I get free help starting a business?
The City's Economic Development Dept (619-236-6700) offers guidance, and you can also reach out to
the Small Business Development Center (SBDC), SCORE San Diego, or the SBA San Diego District
Office -- all offer free one-on-one counseling for new and existing businesses.
