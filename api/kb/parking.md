## Overview

The Parking Division handles metered and permit parking, citations, and the rules for what can
legally sit on a residential street; the police department's Vehicle Abatement Unit handles
abandoned vehicles. Citations can be paid or contested online with the citation number or your
plate and state -- don't pay one you plan to contest, since payment closes the dispute window.
Meters in the busiest districts (Downtown, Uptown, Mid-City, Pacific Beach) run $2.50/hour
(estimate -- rates are adjusted periodically), typically enforced Monday-Saturday 8:00 a.m.-6:00
p.m., later in the Downtown Hospitality Zone and Hillcrest Commercial Zone; always defer to the
posted sign over any general rule here. Residents can get an annual residential parking permit
in a permit district, and there's a legal path for overnight RV parking (a paid temporary
permit) even though oversized vehicles are otherwise restricted overnight. Street sweeping signs
only cover a minority of swept blocks, and MTS trolley/bus station parking is a separate regional
service, not a City program.

## How it works

1. Look up whether your address is in a residential permit district, has posted street-sweeping
   restrictions, or falls in a paid-meter zone using the tools/maps on sandiego.gov/parking.
2. For a citation, pay online with the citation number or plate+state, or file an Administrative
   Review request if you intend to contest it -- do this before paying, not after.
3. For an oversized vehicle (RV, trailer, boat, over 27 ft long and 7 ft tall) that needs
   overnight street parking, apply for a Temporary Overnight RV Permit (TORVP) online rather than
   risk a citation between 2:00-6:00 a.m.
4. To report a vehicle that hasn't moved in more than 72 hours, file online through Get It Done
   or call SDPD Vehicle Abatement.
5. For free transit-adjacent parking, check MTS's own station parking rules (24-hour limit, no
   overnight RVs) -- that's an MTS lot, not a City-managed space.

## FAQ

### Q: How do I pay a parking citation?
Pay online using your citation number, or your license plate number and state, through the
citation payment portal linked from sandiego.gov/parking/citations. Unpaid citations become
delinquent, add penalties and interest, and can eventually put a hold on your vehicle
registration, so don't let one sit.

### Q: How do I contest a parking citation?
File a Request for Administrative Review online before you pay -- paying a citation is treated as
an admission of liability and closes your ability to dispute it. The review portal is linked from
the same citations page; keep your citation number handy.

### Q: What happens if I just don't pay a citation?
It becomes delinquent and accrues additional penalties and interest, and can be referred to the
City Treasurer's collections process or result in a hold on your vehicle registration with the
DMV. Low-income payment plans are available if cost is the barrier -- ask before it goes
delinquent, not after.

### Q: How much are parking meters and when are they enforced?
In the busiest commercial districts (Downtown, Uptown, Mid-City, Pacific Beach) meters run
roughly $2.50/hour (estimate), generally enforced Monday-Saturday 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., with
later hours (up to 8:00 or 10:00 p.m., estimate) in Downtown's Hospitality Zone and the Hillcrest
Commercial Zone. Rates and hours vary by zone and change periodically, so always check the
posted meter or sign rather than assume.

### Q: How do I get a residential parking permit?
Apply online if your address is inside an established residential permit district. The annual
permit costs $11.50 (estimate); a two-week temporary permit is $9. Most addresses can hold up to
four permits (three resident decals plus one visitor permit, or four decals), and all delinquent
citations on the account must be paid before a permit is issued.

### Q: Can I park my RV, trailer, or boat on the street overnight?
Not by default. San Diego's Neighborhood Parking Protection Ordinance bars any vehicle (including
trailers or loads) over 27 feet long and 7 feet tall from parking on a public street between 2:00
and 6:00 a.m., and within 50 feet of an intersection at any time. You need a permit to park
overnight legally -- see the next question.

### Q: Is there a legal way to park my RV overnight anyway?
Yes -- the Temporary Overnight RV Permit (TORVP), applied for online with proof of residency.
Each 24-hour permit costs $1.50 (estimate) and covers up to three consecutive days; a single
address can use up to 72 permits a year. Fees are non-refundable, so only apply for the days you
actually need.

### Q: How do I know if my street has street-sweeping parking restrictions?
Check the interactive street sweeping map for your address. Only a minority of swept blocks
(roughly the Downtown, Mission Beach, Ocean Beach, La Jolla, and North Park areas, among others)
post "no parking" signs with a day and time window -- cars parked there during that window can be
ticketed. Most swept blocks have no sign and aren't ticketed for sweeping itself.

### Q: How do I report an abandoned vehicle?
A vehicle left parked in one spot for more than 72 consecutive hours without being driven at
least a tenth of a mile is considered abandoned under City rules. Report it online through Get It
Done, or call SDPD's Vehicle Abatement Unit directly; response times can run several weeks behind
during high-volume periods (estimate), so don't expect same-day pickup.

### Q: Can I park for free near a trolley or bus stop?
MTS (San Diego Metropolitan Transit System) runs free park-and-ride lots at a number of trolley
and bus stations, but parking there is capped at 24 hours and overnight RV or camper parking
isn't allowed -- vehicles left longer can be ticketed and towed at the owner's expense. MTS is a
separate regional transit agency, not a City department, so its lot rules aren't set by the City.

### Q: I got a ticket for expired registration/parking the wrong way -- is that the same as a citation for a meter?
Yes -- any parking violation (expired meter, wrong-way curb parking, blocking a driveway, expired
registration tags) is issued and processed the same way as a meter citation: same payment portal,
same appeal process, same delinquency consequences if it's ignored.
