## Overview

The Public Utilities Department bills for water and sewer service through the MyWaterSD portal
and app; gas and electric are a separate, privately run utility (SDG&E), not a City service. Pay
online, by phone, by mail, or in person -- a linked bank account has no fee, a card payment carries
roughly a 1.59% fee (estimate). Starting or stopping service takes a stated processing window
(up to 10 business days to start, up to 7 to stop), so plan a move around it rather than expecting
same-day activation. A leak on the City's side of the meter and a sewer backup or spill share one
24/7 emergency line. Water rates are tiered by usage and rose in January 2026; the residential
trash fee (Measure B) is billed separately through the county property tax bill, not through
MyWaterSD, which is worth stating plainly since residents often look for it on the water bill.

## How it works

1. Create or log into a MyWaterSD account to view bills, set up autopay, go paperless, or check
   usage history.
2. To start service at a new address, submit online (water.sandiego.gov/s/movein) or call
   619-515-3500; to stop or move out, use water.sandiego.gov/s/moveout or the same number.
3. Pay online, by phone, mail, or in person; card payments carry a small processing fee, bank
   transfers don't.
4. For a leak on the street-facing side of your meter, or a sewer backup/spill/odor, call the
   24/7 Water and Sewer Emergency Line at 619-515-3525 -- don't wait for a callback on an active
   leak or spill.
5. For gas or electric service, contact SDG&E directly; the City doesn't bill or repair that
   service.

## FAQ

### Q: How do I pay my water bill?
Pay through MyWaterSD online or the mobile app, by phone, by mail, or in person. Paying from a
linked bank account has no fee; paying by card carries roughly a 1.59% fee (estimate). MyWaterSD
also lets you set up autopay and switch to paperless billing.

### Q: How do I start water service at a new address?
Submit a request online at water.sandiego.gov/s/movein, or call Customer Support at 619-515-3500
(Monday-Friday, 7:30 a.m.-5:00 p.m., closed holidays). It can take up to 10 business days to
process, though existing service at the address generally isn't interrupted while the request is
pending -- start it as soon as you have a move-in date, not on the day itself.

### Q: How do I stop or transfer water service when I move out?
Submit a request online at water.sandiego.gov/s/moveout or call 619-515-3500. Processing can take
up to 7 business days, and your final bill uses a meter reading estimated from your historical
usage rather than an in-person read, so a final adjustment can follow if your actual usage
differed.

### Q: What are the current water rates?
For a standard 3/4-inch residential meter, there's a monthly base fee of $35.53 (estimate) plus
tiered usage charges: about $8.51 per HCF for the first 10 HCF used, $9.50/HCF for the next 12
(11-22 HCF), and $11.89/HCF above 22 HCF (one HCF is about 748 gallons). Multi-family accounts pay
a flat rate, roughly $9.96/HCF (estimate). Larger meters have a higher base fee.

### Q: Why did my water bill go up?
Rates increased on January 1, 2026 as the first year of a multi-year adjustment plan the City
Council approved to fund infrastructure and supply costs; the exact multi-year schedule is subject
to change; year-over-year, expect a noticeably higher bill than the prior January if your usage
was similar (estimate on magnitude -- your bill's rate detail is the authoritative source).

### Q: I have a water leak -- is that my responsibility or the City's?
It depends which side of the meter it's on. A leak on the City's side (typically the
street-facing portion of the service line) should be reported to the 24/7 Water and Sewer
Emergency Line at 619-515-3525. A leak on your side of the meter -- inside your home or yard -- is
the property owner's responsibility to fix.

### Q: I have a sewer backup or smell sewage -- who do I call?
Call the same 24/7 Water and Sewer Emergency Line, 619-515-3525, right away -- don't wait for a
non-emergency queue. Backups can come from a blockage in the private lateral (owner's
responsibility) or a problem in the City main (City's responsibility); the emergency line sorts
that out on the call.

### Q: Is my trash fee part of my water bill?
No. The residential trash collection fee created by Measure B is billed through the county
property tax bill (roughly half the annual amount on each of the two yearly installments), not
through MyWaterSD -- you won't find it as a line item on your water bill.

### Q: Who do I contact about my gas or electric bill?
Gas and electric service in San Diego is provided by SDG&E, a private utility -- not the City.
Contact SDG&E directly to start/stop service, report an outage, or ask about your bill; the City's
Public Utilities Department only handles water and sewer.

### Q: Is there help if I can't afford my water bill?
The City's H2O SD program offers qualifying low-income households a credit toward their water
bill, roughly $100 per year (estimate -- confirm current amount and eligibility at
sandiego.gov/H2Osd). A separate, federally funded program can help pay down an overdue balance
for eligible households through county-designated providers; ask MyWaterSD customer support which
option fits before your account goes delinquent.

### Q: The water out of my tap is brown, cloudy or smells bad -- what do I do?
Do not drink it. Report discoloured, cloudy or odorous tap water to Public Utilities Customer
Support at 619-515-3500, which takes water quality calls as well as billing ones; the contact
details are published at sandiego.gov/public-utilities/customer-support. Customer Support is open
Monday to Friday, 7:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., and closed on holidays. This is not a Get It Done
report. A burst main, an active leak on the street side of the meter, or a sewer backup is an
emergency rather than a quality complaint, and that goes to the 24/7 Water and Sewer Emergency
Line at 619-515-3525 instead.

### Q: How do I read my meter or check for a hidden leak myself?
Your account's usage history in MyWaterSD shows a spike if a leak is running continuously, which
is the easiest first check. A physical meter check (recording the reading, waiting an hour with
no water use, and reading it again) can also confirm a leak; if usage climbed with no known cause,
report it rather than guess.
