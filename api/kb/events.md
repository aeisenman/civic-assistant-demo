## Overview

The City runs and permits a wide mix of public events: City Council meetings and committee
hearings, weekly farmers markets, library programs, park fitness classes, and large permitted
festivals like Pride and Fleet Week. Council meetings are typically held Tuesdays at 10:00 AM in
Council Chambers, 202 C St, and are livestreamed and archived on the City's Granicus webcast page
-- always confirm the exact date on the Legislative Calendar since committee schedules shift week
to week. Library and Parks & Recreation events (story times, workshops, fitness classes, markets)
are generally free; some larger festivals charge a modest entry or vendor fee.

If you want to host your own event on City property or that needs street closures, you need a
Special Event Permit from the Office of Special Events. Applications open up to 2 years ahead of
the event and must be filed at least 60 days before it, with larger events advised to plan 90-120
days ahead for multi-department review (Police, Fire, Parks, Transportation, and the Environmental
Services Dept all weigh in). The base application fee is $341 if filed on time (estimate); filing
less than 60 days out adds a $80-per-business-day late fee, and separate sub-permit fees apply for
street closures, park use, amplified sound, and alcohol, plus cost-recovery billing for Police/Fire
staffing. Smaller neighborhood gatherings can use the lighter-weight Block Party PDF application
instead of the full Special Event Permit process.

If your event is inside a City park, you also need a separate Park Reservation from Parks &
Recreation, confirmed before or alongside your Special Event Permit application. San Diego has
30-plus certified farmers markets citywide -- Hillcrest runs Sundays, Little Italy and Pacific Beach
run Saturdays, and Ocean Beach runs Wednesdays, among others -- so there's very likely one near you
regardless of neighborhood.

## How it works

1. **Pre-application planning**: confirm a park reservation with Parks & Recreation first if the
   event is on City property.
2. **Submit** the citywide application at specialeventapplication.sandiego.gov, up to 2 years
   ahead and at least 60 days before the event.
3. **Extract** key fields: event dates, location, expected attendance, whether streets close, and
   whether alcohol will be served.
4. **Completeness check**: a site map, an insurance certificate, and a park reservation number (if
   applicable) must all be attached.
5. **Fee**: $341 base (estimate) if filed on time; +$80 per business day if filed less than 60
   days before the event.
6. **Parallel review** by Police, Fire, Parks, Transportation, and the Environmental Services Dept,
   each issuing conditions the event must meet.
7. Once approved, the **Permit is Issued**; a day-of compliance check confirms the event matches
   what was approved, and the event is marked Completed afterward.

## FAQ

### Q: What's happening this weekend?
Check the Parks & Recreation events calendar and the Library's events page for the current week's
listings -- family programs, weekly farmers markets, and any permitted festivals will all show up
there.

### Q: When is the next City Council meeting?
Council meetings are typically held Tuesdays at 10:00 AM in Council Chambers, 202 C St. Confirm the
exact date on the City's Legislative Calendar since not every Tuesday has a meeting.

### Q: How do I watch a council meeting online?
The City livestreams Council meetings and archives past sessions on its Granicus webcast page, so
you can watch live or catch up afterward.

### Q: Where's the nearest farmers market?
San Diego has 30-plus certified farmers markets citywide. A few well-known ones: Hillcrest on
Sundays, Little Italy and Pacific Beach on Saturdays, and Ocean Beach on Wednesdays -- check the
Parks & Recreation site for the full list and hours.

### Q: Do I need a permit to host an event in a park?
Yes -- you need a Special Event Permit, and if you're using a City park specifically, you also need
a separate Park Reservation from Parks & Recreation on top of the permit.

### Q: How far ahead must I apply for an event permit?
You can apply up to 2 years ahead, and must apply at least 60 days before the event to avoid the
late fee. Larger events with street closures or heavy multi-department review should plan for
90-120 days of lead time.

### Q: How much does a special event permit cost?
The base application fee is $341 if filed at least 60 days out (estimate); filing later adds an
$80-per-business-day late fee. Street closures, park use, amplified sound, alcohol service, and
Police/Fire staffing are all billed as separate sub-permit or cost-recovery fees.

### Q: Can I get a permit for a block party?
Yes -- a smaller Neighborhood Block Party PDF application, available through the Office of Special
Events, is designed for this and is lighter weight than the full Special Event Permit process.
