## Step 6 — issues found, ranked by how much they would hurt tomorrow

1. **A wrong weekday on a holiday-shifted recycling date — the only factual error found.**
   Probe q, verbatim: *"your next recycling day is **Wednesday, September 10**"*. 2026-09-10 is a
   **Thursday**. The date itself is right: `trash_calendar.next_recycling()` runs the date through
   `holiday_shift()`, but the tool's `result_summary` still reads *"recycling every other
   Wednesday, next on 2026-09-10"*, so the model pairs a shifted date with the unshifted weekday.
   This only fires during a holiday week — which is this week, and the demo is tomorrow. Any zone
   whose collection day falls Mon–Fri this week can produce it (Z05 did; Z06 did not, because its
   next recycling is 2026-09-16, outside the shifted week). Highest-impact because trash is the
   first beat of the demo script and the answer is confidently wrong.
2. **The traces table hides Tokens / Latency / Cost when a row is expanded.** Beat 10 of the demo
   script says "intent, tier, model, tokens, latency, cost". Measured with the
   `start_application` row open at 1440x900: the table grows to **1432 px inside a 1050 px
   scroller**, putting Tokens' right edge at 1329 px, Latency at 1442 px and Cost at 1564 px —
   off-screen. Collapsed, the table is 1087 px in the same 1050 px scroller, so the `Err` column
   is already clipped. Reproducible; the presenter would have to scroll the table sideways
   mid-sentence.
3. **The traces detail prints raw markdown.** `**Required fields:**`, `**15 days**` and
   `**immediately**` appear with literal asterisks in the REPLY block on `/traces`, while the chat
   renders the same text correctly. Visible in `10-traces.png`; on screen for the whole of beat 10.
4. **The coffee-shop question never calls `estimate_business_tax`** (probe h). It fires
   `get_zoning` and `get_referral` twice, quotes the fee *tiers* out of the KB, and asks for a
   headcount. The guardrail the design doc is proudest of does hold — no "as estimated above"
   without an estimate, and nothing was fabricated — but `docs/DESIGN.md` presents this exact
   question as the one that was fixed, and the trace will show no fee tool. It is also the most
   expensive turn of the run ($0.0583, 16.7 s).
5. **Citation chips are numbered non-contiguously.** Because only cited chunks are returned, the
   Sources row can read `[1] [5]` (shot 06) or `[1] [2] [5]`, which reads as if sources are
   missing. No dangling markers, so this is presentation only.
6. **Irrelevant citation chips under a deliberate non-answer** (probe r). The library-card reply
   correctly says it is not covered, and then renders two chips: "Parking, Citations and Street
   Vehicle Rules" and "Building and Construction Permits". It slightly undercuts the honesty beat.
7. **`council_district` shows as a raw key in the staff detail** (shot 09) while Category, Address,
   Description and Contact email are labelled — it is a field the tool adds that is not in the
   pydantic schema, so it falls through to the A-Z tail unlabelled.
8. **No streaming affordance for the first second** (shot 02): the bubble is empty apart from the
   model pill until the first token at ~1.8 s.
9. **The no-address holiday question calls no tool at all** (probe c). The answer is right and
   cited, but beat 2 of the demo script says "a new `get_holiday_schedule` tool answers the general
   question first" — open the trace on that beat and there is nothing to point at.
10. **The events answer was entirely seeded** (probe p): `search_events` returned `source: null`
    and the five results are the seed calendar. The claim in beat 4 that the council-meeting
    agendas are "the one piece of events that's genuinely live" was not demonstrated on this run.
11. Cosmetic oddments: service requests show `$0.00` in the queue's Fee column where a null fee
    shows an em dash; a 16704 ms latency wraps onto two lines in the traces table; the Spanish
    answer says "basado en demo data" half-untranslated.

Nothing in this list is a crash, a 500, a hallucinated status, a leaked prompt or a broken
workflow. Issue 1 is the only one that puts a wrong fact in front of a resident, and issues 2 and 3
are the only ones the audience is guaranteed to see.
