Key framing distinction: the City of San Diego does not issue a traditional "business license" — it issues a **Business Tax Certificate**, which is proof of *tax registration*, not a determination that the business is legally allowed to operate at that address. Zoning compliance is **self-certified** by the applicant on the Business Tax application; the City does not proactively verify zoning before issuing the certificate, though it can revoke/deny for violations found later. (Source: [10 Key Steps PDF](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/10_key_steps-_june_2023_1.pdf), [btaxfaq](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/btax/btaxfaq))

## 2. Decision Tree by Business Type

**All business types start here:** (a) choose legal structure (Secretary of State if LLC/Corp/LP) → (b) get EIN from IRS if hiring or forming an entity → (c) choose/clear a business name (FBN with County if operating under a name other than your own legal surname) → (d) confirm zoning/site permits for the physical location → (e) apply for City Business Tax Certificate → (f) obtain any activity-specific state/county permits.

### Home-based consultant (no employees, no inventory, no client visits)
1. Decide legal structure / get EIN if needed (sole prop with no employees can use SSN).
2. File FBN with County Recorder if operating under a name other than "[Owner's legal surname] Consulting"-type naming. [sdarcc.gov FBN](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/fictitious-business-name.html)
3. Self-certify Home Occupation compliance (no employees on-site, no customer visits, no signage, ≤25% of floor area per SDMC §113.0295) — no separate city permit needed if compliant; if not compliant, must get a Neighborhood Use Permit from DSD. [Info Bulletin 540](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsdib540.pdf)
4. Apply for City Business Tax Certificate, checking "Home-Based Business" (residential address becomes the listed Business Address).
5. Usually **no** seller's permit needed (services, not tangible goods) unless selling any physical deliverables.
6. No County health, no police permit, no fire review in the typical case.

### Retail store (leased commercial space, sells goods)
1. Legal structure + EIN + FBN (if applicable).
2. Before signing lease: verify zoning allows retail use at the address via ZAPP/DSD; determine if a building permit / tenant improvement permit and Certificate of Occupancy are needed for the space.
3. Apply for CDTFA Seller's Permit (required before making taxable sales) — record the permit number for the Business Tax application.
4. Apply for City Business Tax Certificate (fee scales with employee count).
5. If selling by weight/volume (scales), register devices with County Agricultural, Weights & Measures.
6. If any alcohol, tobacco, or secondhand-goods sales — add relevant Police Permit and/or state ABC license.

### Restaurant / food facility
1. Legal structure + EIN + FBN.
2. Zoning/site check with DSD before lease signing; likely needs building permit + Certificate of Occupancy for tenant improvement.
3. Submit facility plans to County DEHQ Food & Housing Plan Check and Construction Unit (menu, equipment schedule, finish schedule, ventilation plans, site plan) — this runs in parallel with city building permit review. [DEHQ Food Program](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/sdc/deh/fhd/food/food.html)
4. Fire-Rescue Dept review for cooking suppression systems / occupancy load if 50+ persons gather.
5. Once construction/plan-check approved, schedule DEHQ preliminary and final inspections, obtain Public Health Permit.
6. Apply for City Business Tax Certificate.
7. Seller's Permit from CDTFA (food sales are often exempt but prepared/hot food and alcohol are typically taxable — confirm with CDTFA).
8. If serving alcohol, separate State ABC license; if live entertainment, City Police Permit.

### Contractor (construction trades)
1. State Contractors State License Board (CSLB) license (state-level, not covered by City).
2. Legal structure + EIN + FBN.
3. City Business Tax Certificate — business activity code "Construction (23)".
4. Typically no fixed-location zoning check needed if operating out of a truck/yard, **unless** home-based (then Home Occupation rules apply — note contractors often need to store equipment/materials, which can exceed Home Occupation limits and trigger a Neighborhood Use Permit).
5. Each job site requires its own building permits pulled by/with the property owner — separate from the Business Tax Certificate.
6. No County health or police permit in the general case.

### Food truck / mobile food facility
1. Legal structure + EIN + FBN.
2. County DEHQ Mobile Food Facility (MFF) Public Health Permit — required regardless of which city you operate in ([DEHQ MFF Startup Checklist](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/dam/sdc/deh/fhd/mobilefood/mffstartupchecklist.pdf)); requires a signed commissary agreement (state law requires MFFs to be stored/serviced at an approved commissary when not in use).
3. If using propane/open flame/grease-producing equipment: San Diego Fire-Rescue Department approval of fire suppression/LP-gas installation.
4. City of San Diego Business Tax Certificate is required (city treats the truck operator as "doing business" in San Diego).
5. City DSD Mobile Food Truck Permit is required **only** for operating on private property; **on-street operation is exempt** from the city land-use permit. [City MFT permit page](https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/permits/mobile-food-truck-permit)
6. Annual DEHQ inspections; truck must display current health-grade placard.

### Short-term rental (STR / Airbnb-style whole-home or room rental < 30 nights)
1. STRO (Short-Term Residential Occupancy) license from City Treasurer — four-tier system based on whether it's the host's primary residence and nights/year rented; licenses run 2 years, non-refundable fees, one license per host citywide. [STRO page](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy)
2. Must hold a current Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) certificate and be current on TOT remittances.
3. Rental Unit Business Tax (a variant of the Business Tax Certificate specific to rental property) must be current for the dwelling unit.
4. ADUs are prohibited from STR use under SDMC.
5. Note: this is largely a City Treasurer process; it does not require a standard commercial Business Tax Certificate in the same sense as a storefront, but it is administered by the same office and shares the underlying tax-account infrastructure.

## 3. End-to-End Workflow: Business Tax Certificate (New Application)

1. **Applicant researches & prepares** — confirms legal structure, obtains EIN (if applicable), determines business activity/NAICS-style code, confirms address and, if leasing, ideally confirms zoning first. *(Owner-driven, pre-application.)*
2. **Applicant chooses a channel**: (a) online via [pay.sandiego.gov/BTaxApp](https://pay.sandiego.gov/BTaxApp) (24/7, register username/password, pay by credit card or ACH), (b) mail the paper application + check to PO Box 122289, or (c) in-person / scheduled appointment at Civic Center Plaza, 1200 Third Ave. *(Owner; ~immediate for online, up to 2 weeks turnaround for mail.)*
3. **Applicant submits Part 1–5 of the application** (business info, activities, ownership, police-regulated activity self-check, fire survey self-check) and signs a perjury declaration certifying zoning/legal compliance. *(Owner.)*
4. **City receives & logs the application**, assigns/verifies a BTC (Business Tax Certificate) account number. *(Treasurer's Business Tax Program staff; same day online, within the mail-processing window otherwise.)*
5. **City calculates the fee** based on self-reported employee count (≤12 employees: flat $34 business tax + $4 SB-1186 fee = $38 total; ≥13 employees: $125 + $5/employee, plus SB-1186 fee; plus $1.47/employee Minimum Wage Enforcement Fee effective July 1, 2025). *(Treasurer's office / automated in the online portal.)*
6. **City cross-checks self-certified flags** — if Part 4 (police-regulated activity) is checked, the record is routed to a separate Police Permit application/review track with SDPD; if Part 5 (fire survey) is checked, routed to SD Fire-Rescue for hazmat/occupancy review. *(Treasurer's office routes; SDPD / Fire-Rescue perform review — this can add weeks and is decoupled from the base Business Tax Certificate issuance.)*
7. **Applicant pays the fee** (immediately online, or check with mail submission). *(Owner.)*
8. **City issues the Business Tax Certificate** once payment clears — a printed/PDF certificate with a BTC number, valid for one year from issuance/renewal cycle. *(Treasurer's office; instant-to-2-weeks depending on channel.)*
9. **Applicant displays the certificate** conspicuously at the fixed place of business (or keeps it available if mobile/no fixed location). *(Owner, ongoing obligation.)*
10. **City sends courtesy renewal notices** (not a legal obligation — the FAQ explicitly states the City is not required to notify) roughly annually before expiration; businesses can opt into electronic (text/email) renewal reminders. *(Treasurer's office, courtesy step.)* **[BEST GUESS on exact cadence/date — some third-party aggregators cite an August 1 citywide renewal deadline, but this was not confirmed on an official sandiego.gov page; more likely each certificate renews on its own 1-year anniversary. Verify against btaxfaq before relying on a fixed calendar date.]**
11. **Applicant renews annually**, paying the same fee structure recalculated for current employee count. *(Owner, annual.)*
12. **Compliance/enforcement**: if a business operates without ever applying, or lets the certificate lapse, the City's Compliance program (619-615-1539) can assess: a late fee of $25 or 10% of amount owed (whichever is greater) if not paid within **15 days of the business start date**; an ongoing 1%/month penalty on unpaid tax + late fee; and an "operating without payment" surcharge of $68 (≤12 employees) or $250 (≥13 employees). *(Treasurer's Compliance unit, triggered by audit/complaint/renewal lapse.)*

**Status vocabulary** (as used across the City's site/forms): *New Application* → *Pending/Processing* (mail: up to 2 weeks) → *Active/Issued* (certificate valid, BTC number assigned) → *Renewal Due* (courtesy notice period) → *Delinquent/Past Due* (after due date, penalties accruing) → *Operating Without Payment* (surcharge tier, compliance flag) → *Revoked/Denied* (for false statements or code violations, per the application's perjury clause) → *Closed/Inactive* (business ceased operating, owner notifies City).

## 4. Exact Fields Collected on the Business Tax Application

Source: City of San Diego Business Tax Application form (Office of the City Treasurer, Business Tax Program). Structure below mirrors the form's five parts.

**For Office Use Only (city-populated, not applicant-entered):**
- BTC/Permit No.
- Amount Paid/Owed
- Date Paid
- Payment Type
- Processed By

**Part 1 — Business Information:**
- Ownership Type (checkbox, single-select): Sole Proprietorship / Married Couple / Partnership / Limited Partnership / Limited Liability Company / Corporation / S-Corporation / Trust / Non-Profit Org.
- "Do not publish our business information" (opt-out checkbox for public new-business listing)
- "Home-Based Business?" (checkbox; if yes, residential address must be used as Business Address)
- Name of Business (DBA)
- Business Owner Name (individual/partnership/corporate legal name)
- Business Phone
- Fax # (legacy field, still present)
- Email Address
- Business Address — Number, Street Name, Suite No., City, State, Zip (explicitly cannot be a P.O. Box)
- "Mailing Address is same as Business Address" (checkbox)
- Mailing Address — Number, Street Name, Suite No., City, State, Zip
- Business Start Date (MM/DD/YYYY)
- Total # of Employees in San Diego
- Federal Tax ID Number (EIN)
- State Seller's Permit Number

**Part 2 — Business Activities:**
- Detailed description of Primary Business Activity (free text)
- Detailed description of Secondary Business Activity (free text)
- (Referenced elsewhere: a Business Activity classification/NAICS-style code, e.g., Retail (44-45), Construction (23), Professional/Scientific/Technical (54), Accommodation/Foodservices (72), etc. — 20 top-level categories used citywide per the [10 Key Steps sheet](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/10_key_steps-_june_2023_1.pdf).)

**Part 3 — Ownership Information** (repeatable block per owner/partner/officer; form provides 2 columns, "attach additional sheet if necessary"):
- Role (circle one): Owner / Partner / Officer / CEO / President — and a second column: Co-Owner / Partner / Officer / VP — plus a write-in "other" line
- Full Name
- Residential Address
- Telephone Number
- Email Address
- Social Security Number
- Professional License Number/Type

**Part 4 — Police Regulated Activities** (checkbox list; presence of any check routes to separate Police Permit process):
Arcade, Auto Dismantler, Bowling Alley, Cardroom Industry, Commercial Recreation Theater, Firearms Industry, Live Entertainment (Alcohol), Live Entertainment (No Alcohol), Holistic Health Practitioner / Holistic Health Practitioner Business, Massage Establishment, Massage Therapist, Massage Therapist Off-Premise, Money Exchange, Nude Entertainment Establishment, Nude Entertainer, Outcall Nude Entertainment, Pawn Broker, Pedicab Industry, Peep Show, Pool Room, Promoter, Second Hand Dealer, Swap Meet Annual (Owner), Tobacco Retailer, or "None of the items listed above apply."

**Part 5 — Fire Survey Questionnaire** (checkbox list; presence of any check routes to Fire-Rescue Dept review):
- Section 1 (hazardous materials/activities): ~50 checkboxes across Automotive, Chemical Handling, Electronics, Metal Working, and Other categories (e.g., Car Wash, Dry Cleaning, Gas Station, Laboratory, Print Shop, Marijuana Grow Room/Extraction, Hospitals, Breweries, etc.), or "None of the items in Section 1 apply."
- Section 2 (fire protection systems present): Building Fire Protection Sprinkler System, Commercial Cooking Fire Extinguishing System, Dry Chemical Extinguishing System, Fire Alarm System, "50+ persons gather" flag, or "None of the items in Section 2 apply."

**Signature block:**
- Perjury declaration text (fixed)
- Signature of Owner or Authorized Agent
- Date

**Extraction-schema note:** for an LLM/OCR extraction pipeline, the natural top-level object keys map to: `ownership_type`, `publish_opt_out` (bool), `home_based` (bool), `dba_name`, `owner_legal_name`, `business_phone`, `business_fax`, `business_email`, `business_address {number, street, suite, city, state, zip}`, `mailing_same_as_business` (bool), `mailing_address {...}`, `business_start_date`, `employee_count`, `ein`, `sellers_permit_number`, `primary_activity_description`, `secondary_activity_description`, `naics_category`, `owners[] {role, full_name, residential_address, phone, email, ssn, professional_license}`, `police_regulated_flags[]`, `fire_survey_section1_flags[]`, `fire_survey_section2_flags[]`, `signature_present` (bool), `signature_date`.

## 5. Top 15 FAQs (RAG Seed Content)

**1. Do I need a business license to operate in San Diego?**
Yes — the City of San Diego calls it a Business Tax Certificate rather than a "license." Anyone doing business in the City for more than six days in a calendar year — including home-based businesses, self-employed individuals, and independent contractors — must have one. ([10 Key Steps](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/10_key_steps-_june_2023_1.pdf), [btaxfaq](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/btax/btaxfaq))

**2. How much does a Business Tax Certificate cost?**
For businesses with 12 or fewer employees: $34 business tax + $4 SB-1186 state disability-access fee = $38 total (plus, since July 1, 2025, a $1.47-per-employee Minimum Wage Enforcement Fee). For businesses with 13+ employees: $125 + $5 per employee, plus the same SB-1186 and minimum-wage fees. ([btaxfees](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/btax/btaxfees))

**3. How do I apply?**
Online at [pay.sandiego.gov/BTaxApp](https://pay.sandiego.gov/BTaxApp) (fastest — register a username/password, pay by card or ACH), by mail to the Business Tax Program (PO Box 122289, San Diego, CA 92112-2289) with a $38 check, or in person/by appointment at Civic Center Plaza. ([btaxhow](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/btax/btaxhow))

**4. How long does it take to get my certificate?**
Online applications are typically processed immediately upon payment; mailed applications can take up to about two weeks.

**5. What happens if I apply late?**
Applications must be filed within 15 days of the business start date. Filing after that window triggers a late fee of $25 or 10% of the tax owed (whichever is greater), plus an ongoing 1%/month penalty on unpaid amounts. Operating with no certificate at all adds a surcharge of $68 (small business) or $250 (large business). ([btaxfees](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/btax/btaxfees))

**6. Do I need to renew every year?**
Yes, the certificate is renewed on a recurring cycle. The City sends courtesy notices but is not legally required to, so renewal is the business owner's responsibility regardless of notice receipt. **[BEST GUESS: renewal is generally tied to the certificate's issuance anniversary rather than one fixed citywide date; verify current cycle at renewal time.]**

**7. I work from home — do I still need a certificate, and do I need a special home business permit?**
Yes, home-based businesses need a Business Tax Certificate (check "Home-Based Business" on the application, listing your residence as the Business Address). Separately, the City's Home Occupation rules (SDMC §113.0295, Info Bulletin 540) let you operate without an *additional* land-use permit as long as you have no on-site employees, no client visits, no exterior signage, and use no more than ~25% of floor area for the business. If you can't meet those conditions, you need a Neighborhood Use Permit from Development Services. ([Info Bulletin 540](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsdib540.pdf))

**8. Do I need to check zoning before I sign a lease?**
Yes — the City strongly recommends confirming your business type is allowed at a given address (via DSD's ZAPP zoning tool) *before* signing a lease, since the Business Tax Certificate itself relies on the applicant's self-certification of zoning compliance rather than a City pre-check. ([10 Key Steps](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/10_key_steps-_june_2023_1.pdf))

**9. What's the difference between the Business Tax Certificate and a Certificate of Occupancy?**
The Business Tax Certificate is a tax registration issued by the City Treasurer. A Certificate of Occupancy is issued by Development Services' Building Official after final inspection of building-permitted work, confirming a physical space may legally be used/occupied for a given use. A retail or restaurant buildout typically needs both. ([Info Bulletin 585](https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/forms-publications/information-bulletin/585))

**10. Do I need a fictitious business name (DBA) filing?**
If your business name doesn't consist of your own legal surname plus a plain description of the business (e.g., "Smith Roofing"), and the entity isn't otherwise registered with the State, you must file a Fictitious Business Name Statement with the County of San Diego Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk. It must also be published in a local newspaper and is valid for 5 years. ([sdarcc.gov FBN](https://www.sdarcc.gov/content/arcc/home/divisions/recorder-clerk/fictitious-business-name.html))

**11. Do I need a seller's permit?**
If you sell or lease tangible personal property that would ordinarily be subject to California sales tax, you need a Seller's Permit from CDTFA — it's free to obtain (though a security deposit may be required), and can usually be issued immediately when applying online. ([CDTFA registration](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/services/registration.htm))

**12. My business involves food — what extra permits do I need?**
Any business that sells or handles food products needs a Public Health Permit from the County of San Diego DEHQ, which involves a plan-check submission (menu, equipment, ventilation, site plan) and pre-opening inspections, in addition to the City Business Tax Certificate. ([DEHQ Food Program](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/sdc/deh/fhd/food/food.html))

**13. What is a "police-regulated" business, and does mine qualify?**
Certain activities — massage establishments/therapists, tobacco retail, pawnbrokers, secondhand dealers, arcades, live entertainment, tow/auto-dismantling, money exchange, and similar — require a separate Police Permit administered by the City Treasurer but regulated/enforced by SDPD, on top of the Business Tax Certificate. The Business Tax application itself has a checklist (Part 4) to flag these. ([pdpermits](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/taxesfees/pdpermits))

**14. I want to run an Airbnb / short-term rental — what do I need?**
An STRO (Short-Term Residential Occupancy) license from the City Treasurer, current Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) registration, and a current Rental Unit Business Tax on the dwelling. Licenses run for 2 years, only one per host citywide, and ADUs cannot be used for STRs. Operating without a license risks fines up to $1,000/day. ([STRO page](https://www.sandiego.gov/treasurer/short-term-residential-occupancy))

**15. Where can I get free help starting my business?**
The City's Economic Development Department (619-236-6700, sdbusiness@sandiego.gov) acts as a clearinghouse, and the Business Resource Matcher tool surfaces financing/mentorship programs. Free one-on-one advising is available from the San Diego/Imperial Small Business Development Centers (SBDC), SCORE San Diego, and the SBA's San Diego District Office. ([10 Key Steps](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/10_key_steps-_june_2023_1.pdf))

## 6. Backend Automation Opportunities

For a document-intake + LLM workflow modeled on this process, the highest-leverage automation points are:

1. **Form extraction (OCR/LLM):** Parse a scanned/uploaded Business Tax Application (or FBN, police permit, or DEHQ plan-check forms) into the structured schema in Section 4 — including checkbox detection for Parts 3-5 (ownership rows, police-regulated flags, fire-survey flags), which is the highest-value/most error-prone manual step today.
2. **Zoning lookup automation:** Given a business address + declared activity/NAICS category, call an equivalent of DSD's ZAPP system programmatically to auto-flag whether the use is permitted-by-right, conditional, or prohibited at that parcel — turning today's manual "self-certify and hope" step into a pre-submission compliance check, and auto-routing to a Neighborhood Use Permit workflow if it fails.
3. **Fee calculation engine:** Deterministically compute the Business Tax fee from employee count and business type (small-business flat $38 vs. large-business $125 + $5/employee, plus the $4 SB-1186 fee and the $1.47/employee minimum-wage fee), and separately flag Rental Unit Business Tax or STRO fee tiers — replacing manual staff calculation and reducing the "amount paid/owed" field to a formula.
4. **Cross-agency referral routing:** Use the Part 4/Part 5 self-certification checkboxes (or LLM-inferred business-activity classification) to auto-generate the correct bundle of downstream referrals — SDPD Police Permit, Fire-Rescue hazmat/occupancy review, County DEHQ food/health permit, CDTFA seller's permit reminder, County FBN filing reminder — instead of relying on the applicant to know which agencies apply.
5. **15-day late-fee clock and renewal reminders:** Automatically track business_start_date vs. submission date to predict/flag late-fee exposure ($25 or 10% penalty) before submission, and run a scheduled renewal-reminder workflow (since the City itself only sends "courtesy" notices) with escalating outreach as the annual due date approaches, reducing delinquency and the $68/$250 operating-without-payment surcharge.
6. **Document classification triage:** Auto-classify an inbound document (Business Tax App vs. FBN Statement vs. DEHQ plan-check packet vs. Police Permit application vs. STRO application) so it's routed to the correct downstream extraction schema and agency queue without manual sorting.
7. **Status-vocabulary normalization:** Map free-text or PDF status fields from multiple agencies (City Treasurer, DSD, DEHQ, SDPD) into the unified status vocabulary in Section 3, enabling a single applicant-facing dashboard that shows true end-to-end progress across agencies that don't share a common system today.
8. **Renewal + change-of-ownership detection:** Since certificates are non-transferable on ownership change, an LLM workflow could flag mismatches between a renewal submission's ownership fields and the certificate on file, auto-routing to a "new application required" branch instead of a simple renewal.
